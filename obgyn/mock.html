Labour 19 questions

Q1Past paper 2024-25L43

What is the correct order of the cardinal movements of labour?

Answer D: Engagement, descent, flexion, internal rotation, extension, external rotation

Engagement → descent → flexion → internal rotation → extension → restitution/external rotation → expulsion.

Source: Lecture 43 · Mechanism of normal labour (cardinal movements)

Q2Past paper 2024-25L43

Hyperextension of the fetal head results in which presentation?

Answer B: Face

Maximum extension = face; partial extension = brow; maximal flexion = vertex.

Source: Lecture 43 · Mechanism of normal labour (attitude)

Q3Past paper 2024-25L44

Which is a component of active management of the third stage of labour?

Answer C: Controlled cord traction

Active management = uterotonic (oxytocin) + controlled cord traction + uterine massage. It prevents PPH.

Source: Lecture 44 · Management of normal labour (active management)

Q4Past paper 2021L44

What is the maximum normal duration of the third stage of labour?

Answer A: 30 minutes

Normal third stage is under 30 minutes; beyond that the placenta is “retained”.

Source: Lecture 44 · Management of normal labour (active management of 3rd stage)

Q5Past paper 2021L45

A woman in active labour is 6 cm dilated, and again 6 cm four hours later, with only 1 contraction per minute recorded as weak. Membranes are ruptured. What is the next step?

Answer C: Intravenous oxytocin augmentation

Lack of progress with INADEQUATE contractions = power problem → augment with oxytocin. Caesarean is for arrest despite adequate contractions (4 h) or inadequate contractions for 6 h.

Source: Lecture 45 · Abnormal labour (active phase disorders)

Q6Past paper 2021L45

On a partogram, what is the interval between the alert line and the action line?

Answer B: 4 hours

Line 1 = alert line, line 2 = action line, 4 hours apart. Crossing the action line demands a management decision.

Source: Lecture 45 · Abnormal labour (partogram)

Q7Past paper 2021 · 2024-25★ RepeatedL46

According to the lecture (ACOG), external cephalic version is best performed at:

Answer D: 37 weeks

ECV at 37 weeks: earlier, breech may recur or the fetus may turn by itself and preterm labour may be triggered; later, less liquor and a more irritable uterus.

The 2025 recall says 36 weeks (RCOG offers ECV from 36 weeks in nulliparae). The lecture slide says 37 weeks. If both appear, choose the one in the lecture.

Source: Lecture 46 · Breech delivery (ECV timing)

Q8Past paper 2021L47

A woman in active labour: PV reveals the anterior fontanelle, root of the nose and supraorbital ridges. What is the management?

Answer A: Caesarean section

These findings (mentum not felt) = brow presentation. Its mentovertical diameter (13.5 cm) cannot pass a normal pelvis; persistent brow → CS.

Source: Lecture 47 · Malpresentation (brow)

Q9Past paper 2021L48

An OP position, 7 cm dilated, with 3 contractions per 10 minutes and a reassuring CTG. What is the management?

Answer C: Close monitoring and allow time to rotate

Most OP positions rotate spontaneously (long rotation) while labour progresses and the fetus is well.

Source: Lecture 48 · Occiput posterior (labour management)

Q10Past paper 2021 · 2023★ RepeatedL49

Shoulder dystocia is diagnosed. What is the first-line management?

Answer A: McRoberts manoeuvre with suprapubic pressure

Call for help, then McRoberts (hyperflex the thighs) + suprapubic pressure resolve most cases.

Source: Lecture 49 · Shoulder dystocia (HELPERR)

Q11Past paper 2021L50

A woman in labour has a pulsating cord in the vagina. What must be avoided?

Answer B: Handling the cord with the hand

Handling (and cold air) causes umbilical artery vasospasm. Do not replace the cord; minimal handling, cover with warm saline packs.

Source: Lecture 50 · Cord prolapse (management)

Q12Past paper 2021L51

A woman whose previous caesarean was through a vertical (classical) uterine incision is now pregnant with no complications. What is the plan?

Answer D: Elective repeat caesarean section

Classical/vertical/T-shaped incisions are contraindications to VBAC. Elective CS is scheduled around 39 weeks unless indicated earlier.

Source: Lecture 51 · VBAC / TOLAC (exclusion criteria)

Q13Past paper 2021L52

What is the indication for LOW forceps?

Answer B: Head at station +2 or below, occiput anterior

Low forceps = leading edge ≥ +2 cm; mid = 0 to +2; outlet = scalp visible at the introitus.

Source: Lecture 52 · Operative delivery (forceps classification)

Q14Past paper 2021L53

After delivery there is heavy bleeding; the fundus is soft and rising above the umbilicus. What is the cause?

Answer D: Uterine atony

Soft, boggy, enlarged uterus = atony (70% of PPH).

Source: Lecture 53 · Postpartum haemorrhage (uterine atony)

Q15Past paper 2024-25L54

A woman has a difficult labour and later develops continuous leakage of urine from the vagina. What is the mechanism?

Answer C: Pressure necrosis from obstructed labour causing a fistula

Prolonged pressure of the head on the bladder base causes ischaemic necrosis → vesicovaginal fistula.

Source: Lecture 54 · Obstetric injuries (link to obstructed labour)

Q16Past paper 2021 · 2024-25★ RepeatedL55

A woman at 42 weeks has an unfavourable cervix (closed, posterior, −2). What is used for induction?

Answer A: Vaginal prostaglandin E2

An unfavourable cervix needs ripening first (PGE2/PGE1 or a Foley catheter). Amniotomy needs an accessible cervix; oxytocin works poorly on an unripe cervix.

Source: Lecture 55 · Induction of labour (cervical ripening)

Q17Past paper 2021 · 2023★ RepeatedL56

At 30 weeks, membranes ruptured spontaneously. She is not in labour, has no signs of infection and a normal CTG. What is the management?

Answer C: Conservative management until maturity

24–33+6 weeks, no infection, no labour, fetus well → admit, antibiotics (erythromycin), steroids, monitor. Tocolysis is not recommended in PPROM.

Source: Lecture 56 · PPROM & chorioamnionitis (conservative treatment)

Q18Past paper 2021L57

At a booking visit, which item in the history best predicts preterm birth?

Answer D: Previous delivery at 32 weeks

Previous spontaneous preterm birth is the strongest predictor.

Source: Lecture 57 · Preterm labour (clinical picture)

Q19Past paper 2024-25L58

A pregnant woman is not immune to rubella. When should she be vaccinated?

Answer B: Give MMR postpartum

MMR is a live vaccine: contraindicated in pregnancy; the puerperium is the ideal time.

Source: Lecture 58 · Puerperium & puerperal infections (immunization)

Medical disorders in pregnancy 16 questions

Q20Past paper 2021 · Midblock★ RepeatedL15

A pregnant woman has Hb 9 g/dL and MCV 105 fL. Which treatment will most probably correct it?

Answer B: Folic acid supplement

Macrocytic anaemia in pregnancy is most commonly due to folate deficiency.

Source: Lecture 15 · Anaemia in pregnancy (megaloblastic anaemia)

Q21Past paper 2021 · 2024-25★ RepeatedL16

A woman at 16 weeks has BP 160/110 with oedema and proteinuria. What is the diagnosis?

Answer D: Chronic hypertension

Hypertension before 20 weeks = chronic hypertension (pre-existing), even with proteinuria (consider renal disease).

Pre-eclampsia cannot be diagnosed before 20 weeks (except in molar pregnancy).

Source: Lecture 16 · Hypertensive disorders (definitions)

Q22Past paper 2021 · 2023★ RepeatedL17

Your neighbour (pregnant, 36 weeks) is having an eclamptic fit at home. What is your first action?

Answer A: Ensure a patent airway

ABC first: protect the airway, place in the left lateral position once the fit ends, then MgSO4.

Source: Lecture 17 · Pre-eclampsia & eclampsia (case 5)

Q23Past paper 2021 · 2024-25★ RepeatedL18

A pregnant woman with grand mal seizures uses valproic acid. What will it cause in the fetus?

Answer C: Neural tube defect

Valproate has the highest risk of major malformations, especially neural tube defects (spina bifida).

Source: Lecture 18 · Epilepsy in pregnancy (medications & malformations)

Q24Past paper 2024-25L19

A pregnant woman with ITP and no complications. When are corticosteroids indicated?

Answer C: Platelet count below 30,000 or symptomatic bleeding

Treat ITP when platelets < 30,000 or bleeding; aim for 50,000 before delivery or neuraxial anaesthesia.

The 2025 recall offered 20,000 and 50,000. 20,000 is below the lecture threshold of 30,000, so it would also be treated. 50,000 is only the TARGET for delivery.

Source: Lecture 19 · Thrombocytopenia in pregnancy (ITP treatment)

Q25Past paper 2021L20

A pregnant woman with type 1 diabetes asks how her insulin needs will change after delivery. What will happen?

Answer B: Insulin requirement decreases

Placental anti-insulin hormones (hPL, cortisol, progesterone) disappear → requirement drops sharply.

Source: Lecture 20 · Pregestational diabetes (postpartum)

Q26Past paper 2021 · 2023★ RepeatedL21

An obese pregnant woman attends her booking visit. What should be done?

Answer D: Early GDM screening (e.g. 1-hour 50 g challenge test) now

High-risk women (obesity, previous GDM, macrosomia, family history) are screened at booking and again at 24–28 weeks if negative.

Source: Lecture 21 · Gestational diabetes (GDM screening)

Q27Clinical caseL22

An SLE patient at 27 weeks on hydroxychloroquine and low-dose prednisone is well; anti-Ro positive, normal urine, C3/C4 slightly low. What is the next step?

Answer A: Continue current medications and monitor mother and fetus

Stable disease: continue treatment, monitor maternal labs and fetal growth/heart (anti-Ro).

Source: Lecture 22 · SLE in pregnancy (case scenario 1)

Q28Past paper 2024-25L23

A woman with jaundice has hypoglycaemia and high liver enzymes in late pregnancy. What is the pathophysiology?

Answer D: Dysfunction of mitochondrial fatty-acid oxidation

AFLP: defective mitochondrial β-oxidation (often fetal LCHAD deficiency). Cholestasis = oestrogen effect.

Source: Lecture 23 · GIT disorders of pregnancy (acute fatty liver)

Q29Past paper 2024-25L24

A woman at 20 weeks with hyperthyroidism has difficulty breathing from a substernal goitre. What is the treatment?

Answer B: Subtotal thyroidectomy

Surgery in the 2nd trimester is indicated for tracheal compression, failed medical therapy or suspected malignancy. RAI is contraindicated.

Source: Lecture 24 · Thyroid disorders in pregnancy (surgical management)

Q30Past paper 2021 · 2024-25★ RepeatedL25

At 34 weeks a woman has right groin pain with redness and an increase in the whole-leg circumference. What is the diagnosis?

Answer A: Iliofemoral deep vein thrombosis

Whole-leg swelling with groin pain = proximal (iliofemoral) DVT, 70% of pregnancy DVTs.

Source: Lecture 25 · Thromboembolic disorders (proximal DVT)

Q31Past paper 2021L26

A pregnant woman has a history of rheumatic fever. Which valve lesion is she most likely to have?

Answer C: Mitral stenosis

Rheumatic heart disease in pregnancy is mitral stenosis in about 90%.

Source: Lecture 26 · Cardiac disease in pregnancy (types of heart disease)

Q32Past paper 2024-25L27

A pregnant woman has loin pain, fever and costovertebral tenderness. Which complication is she most at risk of?

Answer C: Preterm labour

Pyelonephritis predisposes to preterm labour and septic shock.

Source: Lecture 27 · Urinary tract disorders (acute pyelonephritis)

Q33Past paper 2024-25L28

What is a normal physiological respiratory change in pregnancy?

Answer A: Increased minute ventilation

Progesterone drives ventilation: ↑ tidal volume and minute ventilation, ↓ FRC, ↓ PaCO2.

Source: Lecture 28 · Asthma in pregnancy (physiological respiratory changes)

Q34Clinical caseL29

One week after delivery, a primigravida is tearful with crying spells, poor appetite and poor sleep but copes with daily activities. Diagnosis?

Answer B: Baby blues

Onset within days, peaks day 4–5, resolves within 2 weeks, no functional impairment.

Source: Lecture 29 · Psychiatric disorders in pregnancy (case scenario 1)

Q35Past paper 2021L38

A pregnant woman involved in a road traffic accident has vaginal bleeding and abdominal rigidity. What is the first step?

Answer D: Maternal resuscitation (ABC)

The mother is the priority; stabilising her is the best fetal resuscitation.

Fetal medicine 13 questions

Q36Past paper 2021 · 2023★ RepeatedL30

A woman at 30 weeks has had an intrauterine fetal death for 2 weeks. Which investigation is most important?

Answer C: Coagulation profile

A retained dead fetus releases thromboplastin → risk of DIC. Check fibrinogen, platelets, PT/PTT.

Source: Lecture 30 · Intrauterine fetal death (detection of complications)

Q37Past paper 2024-25L30

A woman has retained a dead fetus for 5 weeks. Which complication is she most at risk of?

Answer A: Disseminated intravascular coagulation

DIC risk rises the longer the fetus is retained (usually after 4 weeks).

Source: Lecture 30 · Intrauterine fetal death (complications)

Q38Past paper 2024-25L31

A woman has severe symptomatic polyhydramnios with shortness of breath. What is the management?

Answer B: Amnioreduction

Amniodrainage of 1–2 L over 20–30 min relieves maternal symptoms; treat the cause.

Source: Lecture 31 · Amniotic fluid disorders (management)

Q39Past paper MidblockL31

A woman with an AFI of 2.1 cm has been taking a medication for hypertension. Which drug is the likely cause?

Answer D: ACE inhibitor

ACE inhibitors/ARBs (and NSAIDs) reduce fetal renal perfusion → oligohydramnios.

Source: Lecture 31 · Amniotic fluid disorders (causes of oligohydramnios)

Q40Past paper 2024-25L34

A pregnant woman is HBsAg positive. What does the baby need at birth?

Answer B: Hepatitis B vaccine and hepatitis B immunoglobulin

Vaccine + HBIG within 12 h reduce transmission by about 95%. Breastfeeding is allowed.

Source: Lecture 34 · Perinatal (TORCH) infections (HBV)

Q41Past paper 2024-25L34

A pregnant woman was exposed to rubella and tests show maternal infection. How is fetal infection confirmed?

Answer D: Amniocentesis with PCR for rubella virus

Fetal infection is confirmed by viral PCR on amniotic fluid.

Source: Lecture 34 · Perinatal (TORCH) infections (rubella diagnosis)

Q42Past paper 2023 · 2024-25★ RepeatedL35

At 34 weeks, a woman with chronic hypertension has FGR, AFI 4 cm and BPP 8/10. What is the next step?

Answer A: Colour Doppler study of the umbilical artery

Doppler decides timing: AEDV/REDV → deliver; preserved flow → surveillance (oligohydramnios may still prompt delivery).

A 2025 recall listed “steroids + MgSO4” for a similar stem. Doppler comes first when it is offered in the options.

Source: Lecture 35 · IUGR (FGR) (management)

Q43Past paper 2021L35

An IUGR fetus has DECREASED (but present) diastolic flow on umbilical Doppler at 32 weeks. What is the plan?

Answer C: Corticosteroids and twice-weekly Doppler surveillance

Present but reduced EDV → steroids and close surveillance; deliver by 37 weeks.

Source: Lecture 35 · IUGR (FGR) (management)

Q44Past paper 2023 · 2024-25★ RepeatedL36

Answer B: Chronic fetal asphyxia from placental insufficiency

AEDF/REDF = high placental resistance → chronic hypoxia; deliver (CS).

Source: Lecture 36 · Assessment of fetal well-being (Doppler indications)

Q45Past paper 2023L37

Late decelerations on a CTG indicate:

Answer D: Uteroplacental insufficiency

Late decels start after the contraction peak and reflect reduced placental oxygen exchange.

Source: Lecture 37 · CTG (types of deceleration)

Q46Past paper MidblockL39

Which neonatal complication is particularly associated with post-term pregnancy?

Answer C: Meconium aspiration syndrome

Post-term → meconium passage + oligohydramnios → aspiration.

Source: Lecture 39 · Post-term pregnancy (fetal risks)

Q47Past paper 2021 · 2023★ RepeatedL41

If the incidence of twins in a population is 1:100, what is the incidence of triplets (Hellin’s law)?

Answer A: 1:10,000

Triplets = (1/n)²; twins 1:89 → triplets 1:89².

Source: Lecture 41 · Multiple pregnancy (incidence)

Q48Past paper 2021 · 2024-25★ RepeatedL42

A Rh-negative woman has an anti-D titre of 1:32 at 24 weeks. What is the next step?

Answer D: Middle cerebral artery Doppler (peak systolic velocity)

Above the critical titre (1:8–1:16) → MCA-PSV every 1–2 weeks to detect fetal anaemia.

Source: Lecture 42 · Rhesus isoimmunisation (fetal assessment)

Q10AMBOSS formativeExam trapL5

A 17-year-old cannot actively abduct his right arm from 0 to 15°, but once the arm is passively lifted to 15° he can raise it above his head. The weak muscle is supplied by which nerve?

Answer B: Suprascapular nerve

Supraspinatus initiates the first 15° of abduction and is supplied by the suprascapular nerve (C5–C6). Deltoid (axillary nerve) takes over from 15° to 90°, then trapezius and serratus anterior rotate the scapula.

“Weak abduction” makes students pick the axillary nerve. Weakness only in the first 15° = supraspinatus = suprascapular nerve.

Source: AMBOSS Formative Quiz (M1-C&A-F1-26/27)

Q16AMBOSS formativeClinical caseL8

After radical mastectomy with axillary node dissection, a woman cannot comb her hair or abduct the arm above 90°, and the medial border of her scapula protrudes when she pushes against a wall. Which nerve was injured?

Answer D: Long thoracic nerve

The long thoracic nerve (C5–C7) runs on the outer surface of serratus anterior on the medial wall of the axilla. Serratus anterior paralysis gives a winged scapula and loss of upward rotation, so the arm cannot go above horizontal. Thoracodorsal injury weakens latissimus dorsi (extension, adduction).

Source: AMBOSS Formative Quiz (M1-C&A-F1-26/27)

Q17AMBOSS formativeClinical caseL9

A climber falls and cannot move his right shoulder. The arm is externally rotated and slightly abducted, the rounded contour of the shoulder is lost, and the humeral head is felt below the coracoid process. Sensation is most likely reduced over which area?

Answer C: Lateral aspect of the shoulder

Anterior dislocation stretches the axillary nerve, causing numbness over the lower deltoid (regimental badge area). Lateral forearm = musculocutaneous; medial arm = medial cutaneous nerve of the arm / intercostobrachial; dorsum of forearm and hand = radial.

Source: AMBOSS Formative Quiz (M1-C&A-F1-26/27)

Q18AMBOSS formativeClinical caseL9

An 11-year-old falls off her bicycle onto her right side. She has swelling and tenderness over the right clavicle with intact skin, a normal radial pulse and normal sensation. X-ray shows a midshaft clavicle fracture. Apart from analgesia, what is the best next step?

Answer D: A simple shoulder sling

Uncomplicated midshaft clavicle fractures in children heal well in a sling. Surgery is kept for open fractures, skin tenting, or nerve or vessel injury. The lateral fragment is pulled down by the weight of the arm and medially by pectoralis major; the medial fragment is pulled up by sternocleidomastoid.

Source: AMBOSS Formative Quiz (M1-C&A-F1-26/27)

Source: Past paper 2025 Q26 · 2024 Q12/Q43

Q21Past paper 2023 · 2024 · 2025★ RepeatedL11

Volkmann ischaemic contracture is a complication of injury to which vessel in a supracondylar fracture?

Source: Slide “Mechanism – extension type”; Past paper 2023 Q3

Q22Past paper 2023 · 2024 · 2025★ RepeatedL11

Volkmann ischaemic contracture after a supracondylar fracture mainly affects which muscle?

Answer D: Flexor digitorum profundus

Ischaemia from brachial artery injury hits the deep flexor compartment first: flexor digitorum profundus and flexor pollicis longus.

The 2025 student answer key says pronator teres. Standard references give the deep flexors (FDP, FPL). If your doctor taught otherwise, follow the doctor.

Source: Past paper 2025 Q7 · 2024 Q23 · 2023 Q3 (picture)

Q26AMBOSS formativeClinical caseL23

Red blood cells whose haemoglobin has been destroyed cannot make new haem. The lack of which structure best explains this?

Answer C: Mitochondria

Haem synthesis starts and finishes in the mitochondria (ALA synthase, ferrochelatase). Mature RBCs have no mitochondria, nucleus or ribosomes, so they cannot make haem or proteins.

Source: AMBOSS Formative Quiz (M1-C&A-F1-26/27)

Q28AMBOSS formativeClinical caseL24

At autopsy, a 79-year-old man’s heart shows normal ventricles and a sigmoid-shaped septum. The microscope shows yellow-brown granules next to the nuclei of the cardiomyocytes. What is the most likely origin of these granules?

Answer A: Oxidation of phospholipid molecules

Lipofuscin is the “wear-and-tear” pigment of ageing. It forms from undigested, peroxidised (oxidised) membrane lipids inside residual bodies (lysosomes) and collects around the nucleus in heart, liver and neurons.

Q44AMBOSS formativeClinical caseL16

A carrier protein made from the same precursor as its hormone transports that hormone from the supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei to its destination. The hormone is most likely responsible for:

Answer D: Upregulation of renal aquaporin-2 channels

The carrier is neurophysin. The hormones are ADH and oxytocin, made in the hypothalamus and carried down the hypothalamo-hypophyseal tract to the posterior pituitary. ADH acts on V2 receptors to insert aquaporin-2. Oxytocin stimulates, not suppresses, uterine contraction.

Source: AMBOSS Formative Quiz (M1-C&A-F1-26/27)

Q45AMBOSS formativeHigh-yieldL16

A researcher isolates a hormone that crosses the plasma membrane easily and binds an intracellular receptor to change gene transcription. Which hormone is it?

Answer B: Thyroxine

Thyroid hormones (T3/T4) and steroids are lipid-soluble and bind intracellular (nuclear) receptors. Insulin, epinephrine and glucagon are water-soluble and act on cell-surface receptors.

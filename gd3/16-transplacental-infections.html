High-yield card read first

Rubella

  • Togavirus: enveloped, positive-sense ssRNA, haemagglutinin spikes, no virion polymerase
  • Adult: 3-day rash, posterior auricular nodes, arthritis by immune complexes
  • Congenital rubella (1st trimester): PDA, cataracts, deafness; infants shed virus for months
  • IgM + = recent infection → amniotic fluid testing; IgG + / IgM − = immune; infants protected by maternal IgG

Listeria monocytogenes

  • Small gram-positive rods (V/L shapes), narrow β-haemolysis, tumbling motility (vs corynebacteria), grows in the cold (deli meat, soft cheese)
  • Internalin–E-cadherin entry; listeriolysin escapes the phagosome; actin rockets spread cell-to-cell
  • Neonatal meningitis/sepsis (blood-borne); immunosuppressed adults; cell-mediated immunity is key

Toxoplasma gondii

  • Cat = definitive host (sexual + asexual); oocysts in faeces become infective after sporulation
  • Tachyzoites (fast, transplacental); clusters in a cell = pseudocyst; bradyzoites in tissue cysts (undercooked meat; reactivate in AIDS → ring-enhancing brain lesions)
  • Congenital: hydrocephalus, chorioretinitis, intracranial calcification
  • Sabin–Feldman dye test: antibody present → tachyzoites not stained (positive)
  • Control by cell-mediated immunity (T cells, NK)

A newborn has a patent ductus arteriosus, bilateral cataracts, and deafness. His mother had a mild rash in her first trimester. Which agent is the most likely cause?

Answer A: Rubella virus

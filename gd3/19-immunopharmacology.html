High-yield card read first

Where they act

  • Steroids: switch off cytokine genes; ↑ glucose, HTN, osteoporosis; taper (adrenal suppression)
  • Cyclosporine: binds cyclophilin → blocks calcineurin → no IL-2; nephrotoxic, HTN, lipids, gum hyperplasia, hirsutism
  • Tacrolimus: same action, more potent, preferred; nephrotoxic, ↑K, tremor, post-transplant diabetes, no gum/hair
  • Sirolimus: blocks mTOR (response to IL-2); poor wound healing, lipids, low counts
  • Mycophenolate: blocks guanine synthesis; diarrhoea, teratogenic → switch to azathioprine before pregnancy
  • Azathioprine + allopurinol → severe marrow toxicity (xanthine oxidase)
  • Methotrexate (DHFR) for RA: once weekly with folic acid; leucovorin rescue

Interactions

  • CYP3A4 inhibitors (clarithromycin, fluconazole, diltiazem, grapefruit) → toxic tacrolimus (tremor, ↑ creatinine) → use azithromycin
  • Inducers (rifampicin, phenytoin, carbamazepine) → low level → rejection; NSAIDs/gentamicin add nephrotoxicity

Biologics & care

  • Infliximab/adalimumab (fully human, -umab): TNF-α → screen latent TB, HBV · rituximab CD20 · basiliximab CD25 (IL-2R) · abatacept co-stimulation · omalizumab IgE
  • Prophylaxis: valganciclovir (CMV), co-trimoxazole (PCP); inactivated vaccines only, no live vaccines
  • Filgrastim (neutropenia), interferon-α (HBV), checkpoint inhibitors (autoimmune side effects)

Q3High-yield

A patient taking azathioprine is started on allopurinol. Which complication may occur?

Answer C: Severe bone marrow suppression

Allopurinol blocks xanthine oxidase, which breaks down azathioprine/6-MP → severe marrow suppression.

Q4High-yield

A kidney transplant patient taking tacrolimus develops tremors after starting clarithromycin. What is the most likely mechanism?

Answer B: CYP3A4 inhibition

Clarithromycin inhibits CYP3A4 → tacrolimus accumulates → tremor, nephrotoxicity.

Q25AMBOSS formativeClinical case

A 28-year-old woman comes to the physician for a follow-up examination. Two months ago, she underwent left renal transplantation for recurrent glomerulonephritis. At the time of discharge, her creatinine was 0.9 mg/dL. She feels well. Current medications include tacrolimus and azathioprine. Her pulse is 85/min and blood pressure is 135/75 mmHg. Physical examination shows a well-healed surgical scar on her left lower abdomen. The remainder of the examination shows no abnormalities. The patient should be monitored for which of the following adverse effects of her medications?

Answer A: Kidney injury

Tacrolimus is a calcineurin inhibitor: nephrotoxicity, neurotoxicity, diabetes and hypertension. Azathioprine mainly causes bone-marrow suppression; gingival hyperplasia is a cyclosporine effect.

Source: AMBOSS Team 224 · CBL Week 1 Session 2 · Q7

Q26AMBOSS formativeClinical case

A 50-year-old woman comes to the physician for the evaluation of excessive hair growth on her chin over the past 2 weeks. She also reports progressive enlargement of her gums. Three months ago, she underwent liver transplantation due to Wilson disease. Following the procedure, the patient was started on transplant rejection prophylaxis. She has a history of poorly-controlled type 2 diabetes mellitus. Temperature is 37.0°C (98.6°F), pulse is 80/min, respirations are 22/min, and blood pressure is 150/80 mm Hg. Physical examination shows dark-pigmented, coarse hair on the chin, upper lip, and chest. The gingiva and the labial mucosa are swollen. There is a well-healed scar on her right lower abdomen. Which of the following drugs is the most likely cause of this patient's findings?

Answer B: Cyclosporine

Gingival hyperplasia + hirsutism + hypertension after transplant = cyclosporine. Tacrolimus shares nephrotoxicity and hypertension but not hirsutism/gingival hyperplasia; sirolimus causes hyperlipidaemia and cytopenias.

Source: AMBOSS Team 224 · CBL Week 1 Session 2 · Q8

Q27AMBOSS formativeClinical case

A 40-year-old man comes to the physician because of lower back pain that has become progressively worse over the past 2 months. The pain is also present at night and does not improve if he changes his position. He has stiffness for at least 1 hour each morning that improves throughout the day. Over the past 3 months, he has had three episodes of acute gout and was started on allopurinol. His vital signs are within normal limits. Physical examination shows reduced lumbar flexion and tenderness over the sacroiliac joints. Passive flexion of the hip with the knee extended does not elicit pain on either side. Muscle strength and sensation to pinprick and light touch are normal. A pelvic x-ray confirms the diagnosis. The patient is started on indomethacin and an exercise program. Six weeks later, the patient reports no improvement in symptoms. The physician recommends a medication that is highly effective for this patient's condition. Which of the following should be performed prior to administering this medication?

Answer D: PPD skin test

NSAID-refractory ankylosing spondylitis → TNF-α inhibitor. Screen for latent TB (PPD or IGRA) before starting, because TNF-α blockade can reactivate TB.

Source: AMBOSS Team 224 · CBL Week 1 Session 2 · Q9

Q28AMBOSS formativeClinical case

A 68-year-old woman comes to the physician for a follow-up examination. Three months ago, she underwent heart transplantation for restrictive cardiomyopathy and was started on transplant rejection prophylaxis. Her pulse is 76/min and blood pressure is 148/82 mm Hg. Physical examination shows enlargement of the gum tissue. There is a well-healed scar on her chest. Serum studies show hyperlipidemia. The physician recommends removing a drug that decreases T cell activation by inhibiting the transcription of interleukin-2 from the patient's treatment regimen and replacing it with a different medication. Which of the following drugs is the most likely cause of the adverse effects seen in this patient?

Answer C: Cyclosporine

Cyclosporine inhibits calcineurin, preventing IL-2 transcription and T-cell activation. Its important adverse effects include gingival hyperplasia, hyperlipidemia, hypertension, and nephrotoxicity.

Source: AMBOSS Team 224 · CBL Week 2 Session 1 · Q1

Q29AMBOSS formativeClinical case

Several weeks after starting a new medication for rheumatoid arthritis, a 44-year-old woman comes to the physician because of painful ulcers in her mouth. Oral examination shows inflammation and swelling of the tongue and oropharynx and ulcers on the buccal mucosa bilaterally. Skin examination shows soft tissue swelling over her proximal interphalangeal joints and subcutaneous nodules over her elbows. Serum studies show an alanine aminotransferase level of 220 U/L, aspartate aminotransferase level of 214 U/L, and creatinine level of 1.7 mg/dL. Which of the following is the most likely primary mechanism of action of the drug she is taking?

Answer A: Inhibition of dihydrofolate reductase

Methotrexate inhibits folate-dependent DNA synthesis and can cause mouth ulcers, bone marrow suppression, liver and kidney toxicity, and pulmonary fibrosis. It’s used to treat rheumatoid arthritis and several cancers.

High-yield card read first

Maternal health programme

  • Components: pre-conceptional / premarital → conceptional care (antenatal → natal → postnatal) → inter-conceptional
  • Premarital: prevent diseases passed between partners (TB, STIs), genetic counselling (consanguinity), blood group/Rh, Hb, syphilis, HBV, rubella antibodies
  • Rubella: measure IgG; vaccine is live attenuated → avoid pregnancy for 1 month · Hepatitis B: 3 doses, delay marriage until response confirmed

Antenatal visits

  • Every 4 weeks until 28 · every 2 weeks 28–36 · weekly after 36
  • First visit: history, exam, fundal height vs gestational age, U/S; ABO & Rh (Rh −ve needs follow-up), Hb, syphilis, blood sugar, rubella titre, urine
  • Birth spacing ≥ 18 months (≥ 3 years after CS)
  • Alarm signs: bleeding, severe headache, blurred vision, oedema of face/hands, epigastric pain, fluid leak, ↓ fetal movements

Tetanus toxoid

  • Only vaccine routinely given in pregnancy (inactivated toxin, 0.5 mL IM, after 1st trimester)
  • Doses 1–2 in 6th–7th month, 4 weeks apart (last ≥ 2 weeks before delivery) → 2 years · dose 3 → 5 years · dose 4 → 10 years · dose 5 → solid (lifelong) immunity
  • Protects mother (puerperal sepsis) and baby (tetanus neonatorum)

Q1High-yield

A woman planning pregnancy undergoes antibody testing to confirm immunity against a viral infection. Which immunoglobulin should be measured, and why should pregnancy be avoided for one month after vaccination?

Answer A: IgG; the vaccine contains live attenuated virus

Rubella immunity is checked by IgG; the vaccine is live attenuated, so pregnancy is avoided for one month.

Q17AMBOSS formativeClinical case

A 20-year-old primigravid woman comes to the physician in October for her first prenatal visit. She has delayed the visit because she wanted a “natural birth” but was recently convinced to get a checkup after feeling more tired than usual. She feels well. Menarche was at the age of 12 years and menses used to occur at regular 28- day intervals and last 3–7 days. The patient emigrated from Mexico 2 years ago. Her immunization records are unavailable. Pelvic examination shows a uterus consistent in size with a 28-week gestation. Laboratory studies show: Urinalysis shows no abnormalities. Urine culture is negative. Chlamydia and gonorrhea testing are negative. A Pap smear shows no abnormalities. Administration of which of the following vaccines is most appropriate at this time?

Answer B: Tdap and influenza

Give Tdap at 27–36 weeks and inactivated influenza vaccine in any trimester. MMR is live (contraindicated in pregnancy); HPV and Hib are not routinely given in pregnancy.

Source: AMBOSS Team 224 · CBL Week 1 Session 2 · Q1

Q18AMBOSS formativeClinical case

A 24-year-old woman comes to the physician for preconceptional advice. She has been married for 2 years and would like to conceive within the next year. Menses occur at regular 30-day intervals and last 4 days with normal flow. She does not smoke or drink alcohol and follows a balanced diet. She takes no medications. She is 160 cm (5 ft 3 in) tall and weighs 55 kg (121 lb); BMI is 22 kg/m². Physical examination, including pelvic examination, shows no abnormalities. She has adequate knowledge of the fertile days of her menstrual cycle. Which of the following is the most appropriate recommendation for this patient at this time?

Answer C: Begin folate supplementation

Folic acid started before conception reduces neural tube defects (spina bifida, anencephaly). B12 is for vegans, iron is given in pregnancy, and a normal-BMI woman needs no pre-conception weight gain.

Source: AMBOSS Team 224 · CBL Week 1 Session 2 · Q4

Q19AMBOSS formativeClinical case

A 25-year-old primigravid woman at 10 weeks' gestation comes to her physician accompanied by her husband for her first prenatal visit. She has no personal or family history of serious illness. Her vaccinations are up-to-date and she takes no medications. She has no history of illicit drug use and does not drink alcohol. Her vital signs are within normal limits. She is 167 cm (5 ft 6 in) tall and weighs 68 kg (150 lb); BMI is 24 kg/m². She tested negative for HIV, Chlamydia trachomatis, and Neisseria gonorrhoeae 4 years ago. Testing for which of the following infections should be performed at this visit?

Answer D: HIV, syphilis, hepatitis B and hepatitis C

At the first prenatal visit, screen everyone for HIV, syphilis, hepatitis B and hepatitis C. GBS is screened at 36–37 weeks; TB only with risk factors.

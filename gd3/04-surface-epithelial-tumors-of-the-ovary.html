Surface Epithelial Tumors of the Ovary

High-yield card read first

Classification

  • Commonest group of ovarian tumours (from surface/Müllerian epithelium); marker CA-125
  • Benign (cystadenoma) · Borderline / low malignant potential: stratification, atypia, mitoses but no stromal invasion · Malignant: definite stromal invasion

Serous

  • Most common surface epithelial tumour and most common malignant ovarian tumour (serous cystadenocarcinoma)
  • Tubal-type epithelium, clear watery fluid, papillae, psammoma bodies; often bilateral

Mucinous

  • Multiloculated, thick gelatinous mucin; tall columnar cells with apical mucin, basal nuclei (endocervical/intestinal type)
  • Large, usually unilateral; bilateral mucinous carcinoma → suspect metastasis (GI, Krukenberg)

Others

  • Endometrioid: glands like endometrial carcinoma, squamous metaplasia; linked to endometriosis
  • Clear cell: glycogen-rich clear/eosinophilic cells, hobnail; resembles renal (mesonephroid) carcinoma
  • Brenner: transitional (urothelial) nests in fibrous stroma, coffee-bean nuclei; solid, firm yellow-grey

Q1High-yield

A 58-year-old woman undergoes surgery for a 9-cm ovarian cystic mass. Histological examination reveals epithelial stratification, moderate nuclear atypia, and mitotic activity. The underlying fibrovascular stroma remains intact. Which is the most appropriate classification?

Answer A: Borderline epithelial tumor

Stratification, atypia and mitoses with intact stroma (no invasion) = borderline tumour.

Q2High-yield

A 63-year-old woman presents with an adnexal mass. Imaging reveals a predominantly cystic ovarian lesion containing several internal projections and solid areas. Histology shows complex papillary formations with concentric laminated calcifications. Which additional finding would most strongly establish the tumor's malignant nature?

Answer B: Definite infiltration of the stromal tissue

Malignancy is defined by stromal invasion; papillae and psammoma bodies alone occur in borderline serous tumours too.

Q4High-yield

A 56-year-old woman is found to have masses involving both ovaries. Biopsy demonstrates malignant mucin-producing epithelium. The pathologist requests further investigation before assigning a primary ovarian origin. Which consideration most directly explains this decision?

Answer C: Bilateral mucinous ovarian adenocarcinomas are strongly suspicious for metastatic deposits

Primary mucinous ovarian carcinoma is usually unilateral; bilateral mucinous carcinoma suggests metastasis (e.g. GI tract).

Q23AMBOSS formativeClinical case

A 61-year-old woman comes to the physician for a follow-up examination. Her last Pap smear 3 years ago showed atypical squamous cells of undetermined significance. Testing for human papillomavirus was negative at that time. On questioning, she has had fatigue and an increase in abdominal girth despite a 5- kg (11.0-lb) weight loss over the past 6 months. She has gastroesophageal reflux disease and Hashimoto thyroiditis. Menarche was at the age of 10 years and her last menstrual period was 2 years ago. She does not have any children. Current medications include omeprazole and levothyroxine. Abdominal examination shows shifting dullness. There is tenderness to palpation of the right lower quadrant but no guarding or rebound. Bimanual palpation shows a small uterus and a right adnexal mass. Further evaluation of this patient is most likely to show which of the following findings?

Answer D: Elevated CA-125

Ascites, weight loss and an adnexal mass in a postmenopausal nulliparous woman → epithelial ovarian carcinoma; CA-125 is the associated tumour marker (supports, does not diagnose).

Q24AMBOSS formativeClinical case

A 40-year-old woman comes to the physician for a routine health maintenance examination. She feels well. She has no history of serious illness. She works as a flight attendant. She is sexually active with one male partner. Menses occur at regular 28-day intervals with a normal flow; her last menstrual period was 10 days ago. She had a normal Pap smear 1 year ago. She does not have any children. Her mother was diagnosed with ovarian cancer at the age of 63 years, She does not smoke cigarettes or use illicit drugs. She does not wish to have any children and uses oral contraceptive therapy. She has no known allergies, and her immunizations are up-to-date. She appears healthy. Her temperature is 37.0°C (98.6°F), pulse is 72/min, and blood pressure is 124/76 mm Hg. Physical examination shows no abnormalities. The patient is concerned about her risk for ovarian cancer. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

Answer C: Perform familial risk assessment using a questionnaire

Average-risk women are not screened for ovarian cancer. With one relative with ovarian cancer, first do a familial risk assessment; if high risk → genetic counselling/BRCA testing. Continue the OCP (it lowers ovarian cancer risk).

Source: AMBOSS Team 224 · CBL Week 2 Session 2 · Q10

Q25AMBOSS formativeClinical case

A 59-year-old woman, gravida 1, para 0, comes to the physician for a routine health maintenance examination. She reports a 3-month history of fatigue, which she attributes to her part-time job as a gardener. Her last menstrual period was 2 years ago. Medical history is remarkable for endometriosis and gastroesophageal reflux disease. Her mother died of breast cancer at the age of 64 years. She takes omeprazole and, occasionally, an herbal sleep aid. Vital signs are within normal limits. Pelvic examination shows a nontender palpable right adnexal mass. Transvaginal ultrasonography shows a 10-cm hypoechoic adnexal mass with irregularly thickened septa. Serum CA-125 levels are elevated. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

Answer C: Surgical exploration and oophorectomy

A postmenopausal woman with a complex septated adnexal mass and raised CA-125 needs surgical exploration with oophorectomy for diagnosis and staging. Avoid FNA (risk of spillage).

Q26AMBOSS formativeClinical case

A 58-year-old nulliparous postmenopausal woman has pelvic bloating, early satiety and 6-kg weight loss. She has ascites and a fixed left adnexal mass; ultrasound shows a 9-cm complex cystic lesion with solid areas and papillary projections. CA-125 is 340 U/mL. Histology shows multilayered atypical columnar epithelium with stromal invasion and scattered concentric calcified bodies. Which feature best describes this tumour?

Answer B: Differentiation toward tubal- type epithelium

Papillary projections, stromal invasion, psammoma bodies and high CA-125 = serous cystadenocarcinoma, which differentiates toward tubal-type epithelium. Coffee-bean nuclei = Brenner; gelatinous cysts = mucinous; glycogen-rich clear cells = clear cell carcinoma.

Source: AMBOSS Team 224 · CBL Week 2 Session 2 · Q13

Q27AMBOSS formativeClinical case

A 52-year-old woman undergoes resection of a large, firm, yellow-grey solid ovarian mass discovered incidentally during a pelvic examination. Pathologic examination shows nests and columns of transitional-type epithelial cells surrounded by dense fibrous stroma. On high- power magnification, the epithelial cell nuclei are ovoid with distinct central longitudinal grooves (“coffee-bean” appearance). No cellular atypia or Stromal invasion is noted. Which of the following is the most likely origin or feature of this tumor?

Answer A: Differentiation toward transitional (urothelial) epithelium

Nests of transitional (urothelial-like) cells in dense fibrous stroma with grooved "coffee-bean" nuclei = Brenner tumour, usually benign.

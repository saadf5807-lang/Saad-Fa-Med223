High-yield card read first

Three tunics

  • Intima: endothelium + subendothelial CT + internal elastic lamina
  • Media: circular smooth muscle + elastic and reticular fibres + external elastic lamina; media has no fibroblasts, so smooth muscle cells secrete its collagen, elastin and ground substance
  • Adventitia: longitudinal collagen type I + elastic fibres + vasa vasorum (more in veins than arteries)
  • Sympathetic → vasoconstriction; parasympathetic (skeletal muscle vessels) → vasodilation; sensory: carotid sinus, carotid bodies, aortic bodies

Endothelium functions

  • Selective permeability barrier (by size and charge) · non-thrombogenic surface; injury releases vWF and PAI
  • Vasoconstrictors: endothelin, ACE, PGH₂, TXA₂ · vasodilators: NO, prostacyclin (PGI₂)
  • Converts angiotensin I → II · secretes growth factors (CSFs, FGF, PDGF) and inhibitors (heparin, TGF-β) · inactivates NE, thrombin, bradykinin, serotonin
  • Oxidises LDL/VLDL → taken up by macrophages → foam cells = first step of atheroma

Arteries

  • Elastic (aorta, pulmonary trunk): thick intima, ill-defined internal elastic lamina, media of concentric elastic lamellae (25 at birth, 50–75 in adults) → expand in systole, recoil in diastole (continuous flow)
  • Weibel-Palade bodies in endothelium contain vWF (platelet adhesion) and P-selectin (neutrophil recognition)
  • Muscular (distributing): prominent internal + distinct external elastic lamina, media mainly smooth muscle → regulate flow to organs
  • Arterioles: 1–2 smooth-muscle layers, no external elastic lamina, control systemic BP; metarterioles have precapillary sphincters

Clinical correlations

  • Abdominal aorta lacks vasa vasorum → prone to aneurysm (true aneurysm involves all 3 tunics; ≥50% dilation)
  • Coronary arteries: more collagen, less elastin, musculo-elastic cushions at branches → atherosclerosis sites
  • Atherosclerosis: endothelial injury → ICAM-1 ↑ → monocytes → macrophages eat oxidised LDL → foam cells + T cells = fatty streak → PDGF → smooth muscle migrates → fibrofatty plaque → rupture → thrombosis
  • Hypertension: narrow arteriolar lumen, thicker media, LV hypertrophy · Raynaud: vasospasm → white → blue → red

Memory hooks

Weibel-Palade = "WP": vWF + P-selectin

The two contents are in the name.

Elastic = Expand & recoil

Aorta stores systolic energy in its elastic lamellae.

No vasa, no safety

The abdominal aorta has no vasa vasorum → aneurysm-prone.

PDGF pulls the muscle

PDGF makes media smooth muscle migrate into the plaque.

Exam traps

Media collagen and elastin come from smooth muscle cells, not fibroblasts.
Elastic arteries have an ill-defined internal elastic lamina; muscular arteries have a prominent one.
Vasa vasorum are more abundant in veins.
Prostacyclin is a vasodilator; TXA₂ and endothelin are constrictors.

Q13CBL / AMBOSS style

Q15Exam trap

Q30AMBOSS Team 225 · CBL

A 56-year-old man comes to the emergency department because of chest pain . The pain occurs intermittently in 5-minute episodes . It is not conclusively brought on by exertion and sometimes occurs at rest . He has a history of hyperlipidemia and takes a high-dose statin daily. His father died of lung cancer at the age of 67 years and his mother has type 2 diabetes. He smokes a pack of cigarettes daily and does not drink alcohol. His temperature is 37.0°C (98.8°F), pulse is 88/min, and blood pressure is 124/72 mm Hg. Cardiac examination shows no abnormalities. He has no chest wall tenderness and pain is not reproduced with palpation. While waiting for laboratory results, he has another episode of chest pain. During this event, an ECG shows ST elevations in leads II, III, and aVF that are > 1 mm. Thirty minutes later, a new ECG shows no abnormalities. Troponin I level is 0.008 ng/mL. Cardiac angiography is performed and shows a 30% blockage of the proximal right circumflex artery and 10% blockage in the distal left circumflex artery. This patient's condition is most closely associated with which of the following?

Answer D: Raynaud phenomenon

Attending tip: Reversible ST elevations, normal troponin, and ≤ 30% coronary blockage indicate vasospastic (Prinzmetal) angina.

  • A. Caused by systemic atherosclerosis (fixed plaque buildup), whereas this patient has vasospastic angina (Prinzmetal angina), which is driven by episodic smooth muscle spasm rather than atherosclerotic obstruction
  • B. A metabolic risk factor for classic fixed coronary artery disease; it has no direct pathophysiological association with coronary vasospasm
  • C. A chronic vascular condition strongly tied to atherosclerotic disease and arterial stiffness, not an underlying trigger or comorbidity of vasospastic angina
  • D. A classic vasospastic disorder sharing the same underlying mechanism of exaggerated vascular hyperreactivity and episodic arterial spasm (often co-occurring with migraines)

Source: AMBOSS Team 225 · CBL W6 S1 Q2

Q31AMBOSS Team 225 · CBL

A 61-year-old man with hypertension and hyperlipidemia comes to the physician for a 4-month history of recurrent episodes of retrosternal chest pain, shortness of breath, dizziness, and nausea. The episodes usually start after physical activity and subside within minutes of resting. He has smoked one pack of cigarettes daily for 40 years. He is 176 cm (5 ft 9 in) tall and weighs 95 kg (209 lb); BMI is 30 kg/m². His blood pressure is 160/100 mm Hg. Coronary angiography shows an atherosclerotic lesion with stenosis of the left anterior descending artery. Compared to normal healthy coronary arteries, increased levels of platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF) are found in this lesion. Which of the following is the most likely effect of this factor?

Answer C: Intimal migration of smooth muscle cells

Attending tip: Angiographic stenosis impairs coronary blood flow, explaining recurrent angina pectoris symptoms (retrosternal pain, dyspnea, dizziness, nausea).

  • A. Triggered by inflammatory cytokines (like IL-1 and TNF-α), not PDGF
  • B. Caused by macrophages swallowing oxidized LDL, a process driven by scavenger receptors rather than PDGF
  • C. PDGF directly signals vascular smooth muscle cells to divide and migrate from the media into the intima to build the fibrous cap
  • D. Platelets adhere via von Willebrand factor and collagen; platelets release PDGF after sticking, but PDGF doesn't cause them to adhere

Source: AMBOSS Team 225 · CBL W6 S1 Q11
